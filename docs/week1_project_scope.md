| **Project Name** | MobiliFAI |
| **Domain** | Student Mobility (Outbound Study Abroad and Exchange) |
| **Problem Statement** | FAI students struggle to navigate study abroad requirements because critical details on deadlines, credits and more are scattered across various UTM and host university websites. A 24/7 AI assistant solves this by instantly providing accurate answers cited directly from official documents. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** A study of Erasmus student mobility across seven European countries identified lack of information about exchange programmes and difficulty finding a suitable institution or programme as barriers to student participation. This suggests that clearer information about outbound opportunities and their requirements may help students explore suitable programmes (Souto-Otero et al., 2013).<br>Source / citation: Souto-Otero, M., Huisman, J., Beerkens, M., de Wit, H., & Vujić, S. (2013). Barriers to international student mobility: Evidence from the Erasmus program. *Educational Researcher, 42*(2), 70–77. https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X12466696<br>**Evidence 2:** A qualitative study of nine UUM undergraduates participating in a semester-long mobility programme in Indonesia identified unclear credit-transfer information, administrative coordination difficulties, and inadequate practical pre-departure guidance. One participant specifically recommended updating credit-transfer information on the university website to reduce confusion (Abdul Aziz et al., 2025).<br>Source / citation: Abdul Aziz, M. N., Suppiah, S. M., & Mohd Yusoff, N. (2025). *Across borders and classrooms: Exploring curriculum through student mobility*. *International Journal of Islamic Theology & Civilization, 3*(4), 192–203. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17429731<br>**Evidence 3:** UTM International provides separate resources for outbound mobility programmes and their application requirements. This supports the need for a centralised assistant to help FAI students find relevant opportunities and understand application procedures.<br>Source / citation: UTM International. (n.d.). UTM Mobility Portal. https://international.utm.my/utm-mobility-portal-2/|
| **Initial Related Work / Existing Approaches** | 1. Human advising (UTM International office and academic advisor) is contextual, but limited by office hours, and the published hours conflict.<br>2. Static web pages, forms and PDFs (UTM International pages, FAI handbook, pre-departure briefing) are authoritative, but fragmented and partly outdated.<br>3. General-purpose AI assistants (e.g. ChatGPT, Claude) are flexible, but not grounded in UTM documents and may invent deadlines or rules. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | Existing approaches are fragmented across many pages and documents, depend on staff availability, and contain conflicting or outdated information. There is no tool that answers a student's question from a fixed set of official documents, with a citation, a warning when sources conflict, and a refusal for questions outside the documents. |
| **Initial Project Objectives** | **O1:** To design a document-grounded AI assistant for outbound student mobility based on five official documents.<br>**O2:** To develop the assistant with retrieval, citations and guardrails that refuse visa, legal and medical questions.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the assistant in terms of answer correctness, groundedness, latency and safety. |
| **Target Users** | FAI students planning to study abroad or join an exchange programme. |
| **Data Sources** | **Source 1:** FAI Academic Handbook 2025–2026 (PDF) — academic regulations and programme requirements. URL: https://international.utm.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/UTM-OUTBOUND-PREDEPARTURE-BRIEFING.pdf<br> **Source 3:** UTM Outbound Mobility Portal — available mobility programmes, participating universities, application procedures, eligibility and deadlines. URL: https://international.utm.my/utm-mobility-portal-2/<br> **Source 4:** JASSO Student Exchange Support Program — short-term exchange scholarship information, eligibility and funding requirements. URL: https://www.jasso.go.jp/en/ryugaku/scholarship_j/ukeire.html<br> **Source 5:** China Link Scholarship 2026 Application Guide, Shanghai International Studies University — short-term study and research opportunities, eligibility, funding and application procedures. URL: https://www.oisa.shisu.edu.cn/index.php/en/index/newscontent/cid/130/id/663.html
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Both help students find answers from official documents, with source links to support the answers and safeguards to reduce incorrect or irrelevant responses. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | This system focuses on outbound student mobility rather than internal course advising, meaning it handles external host-university eligibility rules and requires specific guardrails to redirect visa and immigration queries.|
| **Responsible AI Risks** | **Risk 1** — Hallucination: The assistant invents application deadlines or eligibility rules, potentially causing students to miss opportunities or apply incorrectly. <br> **Risk 2** — Outdated or Conflicting Information: Information may be outdated, inconsistent, or no longer applicable, causing students to follow incorrect requirements. <br> **Risk 3** — Safety and Scope: Students may request visa, legal, or medical advice. The assistant should decline these questions and redirect them to UTM International or the relevant authorities. |
